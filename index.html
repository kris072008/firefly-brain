Connectome Fly

A fly loose in a garden. It runs itself: Hunger builds, its antennae smell the sugar around it, and it turns toward whichever side smells stronger until the taste neurons fire and feeding takes over. Switch autopilot off and W A S D gives you the legs back — until it finds food, which it still refuses to walk away from. Stones and stems are in the way — it feels them and turns.

Drives

Hunger climbs with time and drops when the fly feeds. Nothing here is a plan — the behaviour falls out of a few competing signals.

Antennae

Two odour sensors, a few millimetres apart. Steering toward the stronger side is klinotaxis — how real insects track a smell. Berries smell strongest.

Obstacles

Stones, stems and the fence are solid. A bump triggers a short turn-and-back reflex before the fly resumes whatever it was doing.

Leaky integrate-and-fire neurons, tripod CPG, two-sensor klinotaxis, bump reflex, 0.01 s bins. Autonomous, not conscious — it has drives and reflexes, no inner life.
